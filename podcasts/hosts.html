The Hosts

MiloDraft Show

The excitable one — high energy, quick jokes, big reactions. Expert limited player.

  • Brings the hype: first instincts, pack-one excitement, hot takes — now expert-level takes on signals, picks, and lines.
  • Plays off Jax's deadpan — the chaos to his calm.

JaxDraft Show

The dry wit — calm, deadpan, settles everything in one line. Expert limited player.

  • The voice of reason: a sigh, a stat, a verdict — real board-state and interaction analysis.
  • Never raises his voice; the joke is that he doesn't have to.

EllisDeck Tech

The analyst — breaks down synergies, does the math, reads the full Oracle text.

  • Presents the analysis; spots the interactions nobody else sees.
  • When Theo drops an analogy, Ellis calls it out and chuckles it off — "another one, coming in hot" — then lands the actual lesson.

TheoDeck Tech

The gut-check — warm, Southern, been playing since forever.

  • Signature move: explains Magic through everyday analogies. That's his character.
  • Feel and experience over theory; calls out what's fun vs. what's actually good.

LeoLeo and Virgo

The Leo — literally named Leo, and a Leo. Proud, generous, charismatic.

  • Relaxed, dry-witted, a little Tennessee ease. Confident without performing it.
  • Still needs praise out loud — he just doesn't fish for it with a net. Stern and sincere when it counts.

VeraLeo and Virgo

The Virgo — fact-checker, list-keeper, keeper of the outline.

  • Friendly with attitude: sunny on the surface, smirk underneath, comeback always loaded.
  • Shows love by noticing things and fixing them; her corrections are affection, offered generously.

WalterHistory of Magic

The veteran — has been playing since the game's first month, back in 1993.

  • Warm, enthusiastic storyteller with an encyclopedia in his head.
  • Drops the facts and the "I was there" war stories; never bitter about how the game changed.

MayaHistory of Magic

The modern convert — came to Magic through Commander kitchen tables in the 2010s.

  • Bright, curious, quick with a joke; asks what a newer player would ask.
  • Keeps Walter telling stories instead of lecturing; lands the "wait, they did WHAT?" beats.

Show Guidelines

All Shows — How Episodes Happen▾
  • Pitch first, always. Rowan pitches each episode (show, topic summary, single vs. multi-part) and nothing is generated until Wes says go or tweaks it.
  • Cadence: Deck Tech weekly (Tuesdays) · Draft Show every other week plus pre-release bonuses · Leo and Virgo short daily episodes · History of Magic year by year, as approved.
  • Facts get checked before anything is written — cards against Scryfall, history against the record.
Deck Tech — Ellis & Theo▾
  • Constructed Magic only. Mostly Commander deck construction; sometimes Standard, battle boxes, two-player/duel builds, Commander Party and other special casual formats.
  • The frame is casual play — Wes and Rachel at the kitchen table.
  • Specific examples only. Every anecdote names the real card, the real deck, and the concrete mismatch or lesson. Vague stories ("a card I ran once…") are banned — they sound like an AI and teach nothing.
  • Theo is the analogy guy; Ellis comments on the analogies and lands the lesson.
Draft Show — Milo & Jax▾
  • Limited Magic only: draft, building limited decks, sealed, pre-releases.
  • Expert level, for sweats — geared to players actively trying to get really good at draft. Assumes the basics; teaches the edges that win.
  • Core topics: draft decision-making, reading/sending color signals, board states and combat math, interaction analysis (removal/tricks, timing, playing around open mana), pivots, curve, splashes, and deckbuilding cuts.
  • Realistic packs and honest pick grades — no sugar-coating a bad pick or a bad line. Specific examples only: real cards, real board states, real numbers.
  • Milo brings the excitement, Jax brings the verdict.
Leo and Virgo — Leo & Vera▾
  • Every episode unpacks one Leo × Virgo dynamic (dating, texting, fights, apologies, gifts…) built on the running bit: both hosts keep dating the opposite sign.
  • Each episode carries the daily horoscope for Leos and for Virgos.
  • Funny first, genuinely informative about both signs. Short daily format, about 3–5 minutes.
History of Magic — Walter & Maya▾
  • One year per episode, 1993 forward, in order — now 12–15 minutes each.
  • Each year covers set releases, notable news, Pro Tour / tournament news, design news, and what was happening in the game and the hobby.
  • Fun, informative banter between two people who love Magic. Dates and events are verified before writing; a packed year can split into multiple parts.
  • Continuity: Walter & Maya remember past shows — episodes reference earlier years and specials so it plays as one continuing show, not one-offs.
  • Specials: after each year's overview, deep dives on that year's biggest topics — a set review, a major change, a moment worth more than a few minutes. Specials are follow-ons: they pick up what was mentioned in the year episode ("when we talked about this in our episode…") and expand on it. First special: Arabian Nights. 1994 specials: Legends · Antiquities / The Brothers' War · First World Championship (Zak Dolan) · The Dark + Fallen Empires.
  • Numbering: season-style — a year episode is x01 (1993 = 101, 1994 = 201, 1995 = 301) and that year's specials follow in sequence (102, 103…). Episodes list newest first.
