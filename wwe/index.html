Project Bible

Project Bible v0.2 — goals, scope, mechanics, workflow, and conventions. The card files and decision log carry the latest locks.

WWE × Magic: The Gathering — Monday Night Wars

Project Bible version: 0.3 (updated 2026-09-25: audit resolutions — Cultivate/Farseek restored, Get the Tables! v0.2, BF-009 duplicate resolved; commanders marked locked) Status: 🟢 Active — this file is the single source of truth Working title: WWE × Magic: The Gathering — Monday Night Wars

1. Project Overview

A custom Magic: The Gathering design project inspired by professional wrestling during the Attitude Era and Monday Night Wars (1996–2001).

The goal is not simply to place wrestler names and images onto Magic cards. The goal is to create a **serious, playable Magic product that feels unmistakably like professional wrestling** — translating recognizable wrestling concepts, personalities, factions, rivalries, match experiences, and storytelling into mechanically coherent Magic cards and gameplay.

2. Initial Product

Two Commander decks, designed as a playable pair:

  • Babyface deck — primarily babyface-aligned. Locked commander:

    • Stone Cold Steve Austin (W/R/G — Naya). Theme: The Comeback.

  • Heel deck — primarily heel-aligned. Locked commander:

    • Hollywood Hulk Hogan (U/B/R). Theme: Control the Show.

Each deck also gets a 30-card sideboard of spare cards for swapping between games (not an in-game Commander sideboard — Commander has no sideboard rule).

The two Commander decks are the priority. A larger WWE-themed set may be developed later, but the full set is not the immediate design target.

3. Historical Scope

  • Primary era: approximately 1996–2001 — the Monday Night Wars,

    • the Attitude Era, the WWF vs. WCW rivalry. The era restriction governs the wrestling portrayals and events depicted on cards.

  • Promotions: WWF/WWE and WCW are both permitted. Per the project

    • owner, ECW is also fair game.

4. Design Goals

4.1 Playability

Cards must function as real Magic cards. Respect established design principles: mana costs, color pie, card advantage, tempo, creature sizing, removal, combat, rarity, complexity, and Commander gameplay. Cards should not receive excessive power simply because a wrestler is famous.

4.2 Wrestling Identity

Wrestling concept → Magic gameplay concept. Examples:

  • Building crowd support → building the crowd (Empower)
  • Getting beaten down → losing life / losing permanents / being under pressure
  • Surviving a beating → resilience / surviving combat
  • Comeback → gaining advantages after falling behind
  • Tag team → coordinated creature mechanics
  • Faction → groups of mechanically related cards
  • Championship → Equipment or other permanent representing a title
  • Match → enchantment / Saga / battlefield condition
  • Signature move → activated or triggered ability
  • Finisher → powerful payoff that requires setup
  • Interference → manipulation of combat or other players
  • Betrayal → sacrificing or turning against an ally
  • Heel tactics → stealing, manipulating, exploiting, or disrupting opponents

5. Design Philosophy

Magic first, wrestling second — but both must matter. A card should work as a Magic card even if the player doesn't know the reference, while wrestling fans recognize what it represents. Communicate wrestling through mechanics, flavor, naming, card types, art direction, gameplay patterns, factions, and storytelling.

Avoid "100 famous wrestlers." Every important Superstar needs a mechanical identity — persona, role in the era, signature behavior, faction, relationships, storyline function, in-ring identity.

Mechanics must reinforce themes. A mechanic should create interesting gameplay, support the deck's identity, feel like wrestling, be understandable, have enough design space, and work within Magic's rules. Favor a small number of deep mechanics over a large number of shallow ones.

6. Decision Status System

  • 🟢 LOCKED — formally established; do not change without revisiting.
  • 🔵 APPROVED / PROVISIONAL — preferred direction; can evolve through

    • testing or discussion.

  • 🟡 UNDER DISCUSSION — unresolved design question.
  • 🟠 PROTOTYPE — being tested; not final.
  • 🔴 REJECTED — deliberately removed; may be revisited with new info.

Full log: design/decisions.md.

7. Core Mechanic — Empower 🟠 ACTIVE REWORK (2026-10-07)

Empower is the set's core mechanic, borrowed from Reality Fracture's "Empower Jace N" template and reworked into the crowd engine. Each player builds their own crowd as planeswalker tokens; **loyalty counters are the resource.** Supersedes Momentum, Pop, and Heat (archived archive/2026-10-07-pre-empower/).

  • Empower cheers N — put N loyalty counters on a Cheers token you

    • control (create one if you don't have it). Cheers: [0] add loyalty per creature you control; [−2] target creature connives (the face pump — one hero grows).

  • Empower jeers N — same for a Jeers token. Jeers: [0] siphon loyalty

    • from an opponent's Universe planeswalker based on your creature count; [−2] recruit (the heel swarm — bodies multiply).

  • Tokens: colorless Token Planeswalker — Universe; no more than one

    • creature can attack each per combat; not legendary (copy shenanigans allowed).

  • Floor at zero carries over: loyalty can't go below 0.
  • Three utilization categories (🔵 provisional): Spend (finishers:

    • remove 9/10 loyalty as an additional cost), Check (thresholds: "as long as a Cheers token you control has 10+ loyalty"), Siphon (remove from theirs, put on yours — siphoner chooses the destination).

  • The crowd is attackable and removable by planeswalker removal — the

    • engine is real Magic now. Rebuilds are cheap: Empower is widespread.

Babyfaces: generate → build → protect → capitalize. Earned through combat, resilience, teamwork, crowd support, and comebacks. Deck fantasy: "You can beat me down, but you can't keep me down."

Heels: manipulate → steal → exploit → disrupt. The lie/cheat/steal identity — scheming (Lie), interference and cheap tricks (Cheat), siphoning the crowd's loyalty for their own benefit (Steal).

Full spec: design/empower.md.

Signature Tokens — Cheers / Jeers 🟠 ACTIVE REWORK

Replacing the Pop (SET-001) and Heat (SET-002) token artifacts, archived 2026-10-07. The crowd's eruption made tangible: the Cheers [0] banks hype off your board (once per turn, no stockpiling), the [−2] connive is the immediate rally — "get the guy back in it" — now permanent. The heel's Jeers siphons the opponent's crowd into its own and recruits (loot + body, the nWo keeps growing). Design principle: the crowd is fair game, life totals aren't.

Finisher Framework 🟢 LOCKED

Finisher is a formal instant/sorcery subtype (like Trap or Lesson).

  • Finishers are sorceries by default — earned, telegraphed, interactable.
  • Commander asymmetry: Hollywood Hulk Hogan's finisher is a sorcery

    • (the leg drop: cup the ear, crowd counts, then it lands); Stone Cold Steve Austin's is an instant (the Stunner came out of nowhere).

  • Casting cost: spend loyalty — "As an additional cost to cast this

    • spell, remove N loyalty counters from a Universe planeswalker you control": 9 for sorcery finishers, 10 for instants (the instant premium — flexibility costs more). Spending the tank is the story: everything in the building went into one move.

  • Not every wrestler gets a finisher. Drawing yours should feel like

    • a moment.

  • The subtype unlocks finisher tutors, discounts, and "whenever you cast

    • a Finisher" triggers.

  • Locked vs. open: the subtype, sorcery-by-default/instant-exception,

    • and the 9/10 loyalty additional costs are locked. The exact comprehensive-rules wording is open (see Open Questions).

Tag Team 🟢 LOCKED

Coordinated wrestling teams. Rule (locked 2026-09-23; amended 2026-09-28): cards with "Tag team with [Name]" are paired. At the beginning of your upkeep, if you control both members of one or more tag teams, empower cheers 1 — only once per turn, no matter how many completed teams you control. (The 9/23 version gave heel tag teams Heat instead; Wesley reworked it 9/28: the hot tag always pops. 10/07: Pop tokens retired with the Empower rework.) Implemented as a set-level rule (like the monarch), not per-card triggers. Full spec: design/mechanics.md.

8. The Decks

Babyface — The Comeback 🔵

  • Commander (🟢 locked): Stone Cold Steve Austin, Texas Rattlesnake —

    • embodies comeback, resilience, fighting through adversity, team/crowd support. (Locked v0.1, 2026-09-23.)

  • Colors (provisional): White/Red/Green (Naya) — heroes, unity, comebacks (W);

    • aggression, combat, intensity (R); resilience, creatures, growth, recovery (G).

  • Color overlap: Red is the single shared color with the Heel deck (Grixis U/B/R) — the brawling, aggressive core both decks fight over.
  • Identities (🔵 provisional): color roles, pair behaviors & trio identity — see design/color-identities.md.
  • Gameplay arc: establish the crew → take hits / trade resources →

    • build the crowd → comeback → finisher.

  • Provisional roster: Stone Cold Steve Austin, The Rock, Mankind,

    • The Undertaker, Kane, Sting, Goldberg, Diamond Dallas Page, Booker T, Rey Mysterio. (Not finalized; listing ≠ guaranteed inclusion.)

Heel — Control the Show 🔵

  • Commander (🟢 locked): Hollywood Hulk Hogan, nWo Leader — embodies

    • control, manipulation, recruitment, factions, exploitation, crowd manipulation. (Locked v0.1, 2026-09-23.)

  • Colors (provisional): Blue/Black/Red — manipulation and deception (U);

    • exploitation, betrayal, self-interest (B); aggression and chaos (R).

  • Color overlap: Red is the single shared color with the Babyface deck (Naya W/R/G).
  • Identities (🔵 provisional): color roles, pair behaviors & trio identity — see design/color-identities.md.
  • Gameplay concepts: Recruitment (stealing/turning opposing resources),

    • Interference (manipulating combat), Betrayal (sacrificing/exploiting allies), crowd-loyalty manipulation.

  • nWo is expected to be a major faction/subtheme (mechanics TBD).

  • Babyface: BF-001, BF-002, …
  • Heel: HL-001, HL-002, …
  • Shared/future: SET-001, SET-002, …

Versions increment on meaningful mechanical changes (v0.1 → v1.0). Record fields per card: ID, name, type, rarity, mana cost, color identity, P/T, Superstar, promotion, era, faction, tag team, mechanics, deck, gameplay role, flavor text, status, version, playtest notes. Template: cards/_TEMPLATE.md.

10. Card & Deck Design Standards

Every significant card must answer: Is the mana cost / color pie / power level right? Is it doing too many things? Does it create interesting decisions? Is its role clear? What does it do early, late, ahead, behind? Does the mechanic communicate the wrestling concept? Is it era-authentic? Does it belong in this deck?

Power target: casual Commander bracket 2 — splashy and fun, never competitive-level. The bracket-2 bar applies to every card review.

Decks must be cohesive experiences: clear commander identity, primary strategy, subthemes, ramp, card draw, interaction, board presence, finishers, functional mana base, coherent curve — playable against each other while feeling substantially different.

6. Card design — systematic, with IDs and versions

7. Playtesting — goldfish, then babyface vs. heel

8. Refinement — costs, quantities, thresholds, power

9. Finalization — templating, color, history, names, flavor, art, lists

12. Research Standards

  • Magic research: reputable design/rules sources; corroborate important

    • claims (skeletons, curves, color pie, rates, Commander design, templating).

  • WWE historical research: verify historical claims with reputable

    • sources — wrestlers, factions, championships, storylines, matches, signature moves, timelines. Accuracy matters for cards based on real events.

"Does this feel like Magic, and does it feel like wrestling?"

How the set works

Mechanics

The core systems every card is built on — and the laws no design is allowed to break.

Momentum

A persistent player resource — crowd energy, match momentum, the buildup toward a comeback or a finish. Tracked as a number per player, no inherent maximum. Gained, spent, checked at thresholds, or lost. Conceptual thresholds: 5 and 10.

Face: generate → build → protect → capitalize. Heel: manipulate → steal → exploit → disrupt.

Pop SET-001 · face token

{1}, {T}, sacrifice: gain 2 Momentum.
{T}, sacrifice: target creature gets +2/+2 until end of turn.

The crowd's eruption made tangible — bank it toward finisher thresholds, or crack it free in combat.

Heat SET-002 · heel token

{1}, {T}, sacrifice: target opponent loses 1 Momentum and you gain 1 Momentum.
{T}, sacrifice: draw a card, then discard a card.

The heel mirror of Pop — siphons boos instead of banking cheers. Momentum is fair game; life totals aren't.

Finisher subtype

A formal instant/sorcery subtype. Sorceries by default; Austin's Stunner is an instant (it came out of nowhere), Hogan's Leg Drop a sorcery (telegraphed). Additional cost: spend 9 Momentum (sorcery) or 10 (instant).

Tag Team

Coordinated creature mechanic — partners that fight as a unit. Face-side wording is locked; whether the heel version creates Heat is still an open audit item.

The Laws

  • Tokens are the on-ramp; finishers are the payoff.
  • Face gains Momentum; heel steals it.
  • No self-contained Momentum loops.
  • Finisher thresholds stay 9 / 10.
  • Only 2–3 low-Momentum comeback cards in the face deck.
  • ~30 cards per deck touch Momentum; the rest play normal Magic.

Reprint naming rule locked 2026-09-24

Real-card creatures used as reprints always take wrestling names (used sparingly). Non-creatures keep their original name only if the art can clearly carry the wrestling theme and the name doesn't point outside wrestling.

Design bar

Bracket 2 / real Commander precon. Every card must feel like Magic and feel like wrestling — mechanics first, names second. Wrestlers use “Name, Epithet.” Real quotations labeled real; invented flavor labeled invented.
